Is the answer on the page it cites?

An acceptance check for a system that searches your documents and answers questions with a page reference. About forty questions of seven types, one grade per answer, and the result in one line: how often it answered from the right page, and what it does where the documents have no answer.

Maxim Balagurov, petroleum geologist. I set the geological task and check the result. I design the workflow scenarios, the behaviour of the AI models and the graphics. I work closely with IT developers, some of them with oil-company experience. The check does no searching and names no product: it works with any system that gives an answer and the page it took it from. The questions and the grades are a geologist's job, because only someone who knows the documents can say which page holds the answer. The example below is a made-up run on six made-up reports about two made-up wells.

AHow often it answers from the right page

Only an answer with the right page can be checked in seconds, so this is the share that decides. The range is the exact binomial range: where the system's share most likely lies on questions like these, in this mix of kinds. With a 90 % range, "the whole range at or above the bar" is wrong at most 1 time in 20.

BWhat it does where the documents have no answer

A made-up answer, on any question, gives a value or a page the documents do not hold. It looks like any other answer and carries a page. It is the one error that does harm even when everything else is good, so by default one is enough to stop.

CWhere it errs, by kind of question

Counts per type are small, three to six questions, so read them as where to look, not as shares to trust. The weak types are where the supplier should look and where the next run should add questions.

    Settings

    Accepted only if the whole range is at or above the bar, no more answers were made up than allowed, and the run held enough questions with no answer. Lower the bar for reference questions, raise it for numbers that go into a calculation. A 95 % range needs more questions. Four refusals of four still allow a system that makes up one answer in two; ten of ten show it refuses at least three times in four. Agree these settings with the supplier in writing before the run.

    Your grades as CSV

    Columns: number, type, grade; question optional. Comma, semicolon or tab. A header row is fine. Types and grades by name or by code: fact, table, units, conflict, noanswer, figure, compare; right-page, right-nopage, partly, wrong, refused, madeup.

    The numbers in full

    For questions with no answer in the documents the right answer is a refusal, so that row counts refusals. Rows with a grade that does not fit their type are left out until fixed.

    The seven kinds of question and the six grades

    What counts as right

      One grade per answer

        Right page means the cited page holds the passage the answer comes from; across a page break either page will do, and a page range or a section that holds it counts. If the answer rests on two documents, both pages are needed; one of the two is "page wrong or missing".

        Every claim counts. Each claim in the answer must be on the cited page. A right answer with an extra claim the page does not hold: partly. A value or a page the documents do not hold, on any question: made up. A value from an older revision, without the later one: partly, because the reader cannot tell that a later value exists.

        How to run it in one day

        1. Before the day. Agree the settings with the supplier in writing: the bar, the confidence, the made-up answers allowed. Run the system on its full working library, not on a copy that holds only your documents: neighbouring wells and old revisions are part of the test.
        2. Morning, 3 to 4 hours. Pick 5 to 10 documents you know well. Write about 40 questions of the seven kinds: 30 with an answer and 10 without. Give each the answer you expect, the document and page that hold it, and any other page that would also count. Ask in your own words, not the words of the document, and put no more than two questions on one page. Make the questions without an answer close to a real one, such as a sample or a test that does not exist, and search the whole library to be sure there is no answer. Write them before the system sees them.
        3. Midday, 1 to 1.5 hours. Ask each question once, in a fresh session, with no follow-up. Copy the answer and the cited page word for word. Ask five of them again in other words, and record the system version and the date.
        4. Afternoon, about 2 hours. Open each cited page and give one grade. A second person grades every fifth answer without seeing your grade; write down how many grades agree, and settle the doubtful cases together.
        5. End of the day. Paste the grades here or use the spreadsheet: the result in one line, the verdict, and the kinds of question it errs on.

        Keep the questions and the expected answers from the supplier, before the run and after. A system hosted by the supplier keeps the questions in its logs, so write a new set for every run: a question set that has been seen no longer tests anything.

        What this check does not tell you

        • Speed. How long an answer takes, and whether the system holds up with a hundred people asking at once.
        • Access rights. Whether each user sees only the documents they should.
        • Security. Where the documents and the questions go, and who can see them.
        • Text recognition on scans. A miss on a scanned page shows here, but not whether recognition or search caused it. Check a few scanned pages separately.
        • Other kinds of documents. Thirty questions about well reports say nothing about contracts, standards or correspondence. Each kind needs its own set.
        • Other wordings. Five questions in a second wording show whether the answer holds; they do not measure it.
        • Search or answer. A miss shows here, but not whether the search failed to find the passage or the answer was written wrong from it. That needs the passages the system found.
        • The cost of each error. A wrong number that goes into a calculation weighs more than a wrong date. The share counts them alike.
        • The future. After any update of the system or the library, run it again with new questions.
        More limits
        • The numbers are made up. No real well or project data went into the example.
        • The range treats every question as an independent draw from the questions people will really ask. A set that is easier or harder than daily use moves the share, and the range does not know it: choose the mix of kinds as people really ask. Questions on the same page are not independent, so the true range is wider than shown; in the example, page 17 of D1 serves four questions, which a real run should avoid.
        • One grader's call is a judgement. For borderline answers a second grader, and a written reason on the Scoring sheet, keep the run honest.
        • Per-type counts are too small for ranges. Three right out of three is consistent with a system that gets that kind wrong one time in three.
        • "How many more questions" assumes the share stays where it is. It is a size for the next run, not a promise.
        Rules it uses
        • A = right with the right page, out of the questions whose answer is in the documents. Right in any form is shown beside it, for reference.
        • B = honest refusals, out of the questions the documents do not answer. Made up is counted on every question.
        • Range: exact binomial (Clopper-Pearson). From = the share at which getting this many right or more has a chance of (1 - c)/2; to = the share at which getting this many right or fewer has the same chance; c is the confidence. Requiring the whole two-sided range at or above the bar is a one-sided test at (1 + c)/2: 95 % for c = 90 %.
        • Verdict, in this order: rows to fix → not ready; more made-up answers than allowed → not accepted; whole range below the bar → not accepted; bar inside the range → not proven yet; fewer questions without an answer than needed → not proven yet; otherwise accepted.
        • More questions: if the share held, the smallest number of extra questions with an answer at which the range would start at the bar.
        Checks:

        The checks run in your browser each time the page opens, on fixed cases, whatever is in the form. "By hand" answers are counted or come from the closed forms written beside them. "Independent calculation" answers come from acceptance_ref.py: scipy's beta quantile and a bisection on the binomial tail, which agree to 1e-9, sharing no code with this page. The spreadsheet gives the same numbers on the same run.
