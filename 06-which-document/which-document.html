Which document is right?

Six wells, three documents each, and the values do not agree. The page compares them, sorts every difference by its cause, and leaves to the geologist the ones that need a choice.

Maxim Balagurov, petroleum geologist. On a pilot on a producing-field project I work with historical well data: wellhead coordinates, deviation surveys, picks, core and well files. There, by my own estimate, preparing the historical well data was about 1.5 to 2 times faster than by hand. AI reads and compares well; the decision on which document is right stays with the geologist. This page shows the logic of that check on made-up documents. It does not read scans and runs no agents or language models: the values are typed in.

"Systematic" means the page can name the cause: the values agree once converted. A geologist still signs the conversion off, but does not have to choose between values. A survey that parts from the others is not counted here: it may be a second bore or a re-survey of the same hole, and that is the geologist's call.

CWhat a check of one document would miss

flagged by a one-document checkpasses it, but disagrees with the othersno discrepancy

A one-document check here means each document on its own: depths in order, tops and core above TD, angles in range, KB above GL. It is the usual first step when a document is loaded, and it catches what is impossible inside one document. It cannot see a value that is possible but different: that needs a comparison, with the other documents or with the database.

Try your own well

Type the values of one well from two or three documents, or start from one of the example wells. Leave a field empty if a document does not have it. Priority says which document is the record where no two agree; left as "from the name", a drilling report comes first, then the well file, then a summary table. Depths in metres or feet, as written. X is north and Y is east, the geodetic convention of the example; many databases call the easting X, which is one of the things the page checks.

Tolerances and conversions

Differences up to the tolerance are left alone. The pick window is the largest difference still read as another version of the same pick. Declination is added to a magnetic azimuth to get a true one (east is positive). If a total correction to grid north is entered instead, the azimuths corrected by it are to the grid, and in the export AZI_REF still says TrueNorth: correct that label by hand before loading. The transformation turns coordinates of the first system into the second: shift, rotation and scale, as given by the survey department. The EPSG codes are used only by the export of accepted values.

Many wells at once

Paste a table with one line per document, or open a CSV file: the same rules run over every well and give one summary and one register. Columns: well, document, kind, year, priority, crs, x, y, kb, gl, depths_from, azimuths_to, td, then one top:NAME column per horizon, then core and survey. A core is written top-base and a survey station depth inclination azimuth, several separated by |. A uwi column, if there is one, goes into the export of accepted values. Commas or semicolons between columns; with semicolons a decimal comma is read too. Well names that differ only in case, spaces, dashes, leading zeros (W-04, w04, W 4) or Cyrillic and Latin letters that look alike (С-12, C-12) are read as one well, and the page says so. Up to three documents per well. The tolerances are those of the form above. The file is read in this browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Discrepancy register: the six example wells

Every discrepancy the page finds in the made-up set, with the values of the three documents, the type, a hint on what to check and a field for the geologist's decision. The CSV has one line per value: an id for each discrepancy, the value in each document, the value the rule gives, the status and the decision typed here. Decisions are kept in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.

Accepted values, ZIP: what goes next into a database or a GIS. Wellheads, trajectory and tops as plain CSV with the unit in each column name (metres from the reference KB, measured depth, true north where the file says so; TVD, DX and DY by minimum curvature), a GeoPackage layer of the wellheads, the documents behind each value, and the OSDU or WITSML field of each column with a link to its schema. Every row says whether a decision is still pending on it; a decision typed in the register clears it. EPSG codes and system names: EPSG Dataset, owned by IOGP, terms of use; the WKT definitions of the five projected systems the page knows and their TOWGS84 numbers are as epsg.io writes them, not the EPSG Dataset verbatim, and CC BY 4.0 does not cover them. The GeoPackage writer was checked with SQLite against GeoPackage 1.3 on two sample exports, not opened in GDAL, QGIS or ArcGIS. The import windows of Petrel, Kingdom and DecisionSpace were not checked: their documentation is not open.

Limits

  • The numbers are made up. No data of real wells or fields went into the example. The two coordinate systems, FLD-75 and REG-95, are made up too.
  • No scans, no text recognition, no agents or language models. The values are typed in. The page shows the logic of the comparison and where the geologist's decision sits, not a system that reads documents.
  • It does not decide for the geologist. A typing error, another version of a pick, a core interval below TD and a survey that parts from the others are shown with a hint; which value is right is decided against the original pages.
More limits
  • Coordinates are converted by a plane transformation of four parameters that you give. A change of datum or projection needs the proper transformation from the survey department; this is not a geodetic tool.
  • One declination for the whole set. In practice it depends on the year and place of the survey. Grid convergence is not applied separately: enter the total correction if the database is in grid north. The export still writes TrueNorth in AZI_REF for azimuths corrected that way: correct the label by hand before loading.
  • TVD is worked out by minimum curvature from a vertical start at the KB; below the last station it is continued in a straight line at that station's inclination. With no survey the check takes the well as vertical; the export leaves TVD and TVDSS empty.
  • Survey stations are lined up by depth, not by their order in the list. Where one document has a station and another has none at that depth, the other's value is interpolated along the arc between its own stations and shown with ≈; only the document's own stations can be flagged. Above a document's first station nothing is compared, so a sidetrack survey that starts at its tie-in point lines up from there. Azimuths are not compared at stations below 3° inclination, where they mean little.
  • The tolerances suit copies of one record. Between real archives a core-to-log shift of 1 to 5 m is normal: raise the depth tolerance, or read such a difference as a shift. A one-digit difference below 10 m is not read as a typing error: it is a re-measurement as often.
  • Driller's against logger's depth, and TD against a plugged-back or current bottom, have no type of their own: they show as a constant shift or as a TD no rule fits, with a hint.
  • The rules are tried in order and a value gets the first type that fits. Two causes in one value, such as feet and a typing error, are named as one.
  • A rule for a whole document (feet, ground level, magnetic north) needs at least three values in common with another document and holds when at least 80 % of them fit.
  • The reference is the value two documents agree on. Where no two agree, as with two documents that differ, the document first in the order of priority is the reference: the priority given in the form or the table, or else the name (drilling report, then well file, then summary table). A summary table typed from the well file repeats its errors, and two copies of one error outvote the original: where the record is the odd one on a typing error, a pick, a core or a difference no rule fits, the hint says so. The order of priority is the geologist's call; the page only applies it.
  • One well at a time in the form, up to five survey stations, three horizons and two core intervals. The table takes any number of wells, stations, horizons and core intervals, with up to three documents per well.
How it works
  1. Coordinates of each document are brought into the system most documents use. Two documents that agree within the tolerance set the reference; a document that differs from it is checked against the rules for coordinates.
  2. For each document the page asks whether all its depths are off in the same way: times 0.3048 (feet), by KB − GL (measured from ground level), or by some other single amount (a shift whose datum the geologist has to find). If at least 80 % of its depths fit, the whole document is converted. A document that says GL is converted directly.
  3. The same for azimuths, at stations of 3° inclination or more: if every station differs by the declination, the survey is to magnetic north; if by another single angle, the north reference is for the geologist to find. Then the surveys are lined up by depth: at each station of any document the others give their own station or a value interpolated between theirs. If they agree at the top and part below, with different TDs, one document may describe a second bore.
  4. Then value by value. Where two documents agree, the third is compared with them; where none agree, the one first in the order of priority is the reference. The first rule that fits gives the type. What fits no rule is left to the geologist as "no rule fits".
  5. KB and GL that differ are judged last, once units, datum and bore are known: in feet with the rest of a document in feet, the KB of another rig when the depths differ by just that much, the KB of the rig that drilled a second bore, or else a typing error or no rule.
  6. Tops are checked against KB − TVD, TVD − KB (subsea), TVD itself, and elevations worked out wrongly from measured depth or from GL, with TVD from the survey by minimum curvature. Measured depth would be wrong in a deviated well.
  7. Every discrepancy is listed once per document and type, with all the values it covers.
The types and their rules
Checks:

The checks run in your browser each time the page opens, on fixed cases, whatever is in the form. "By hand" answers come from arithmetic written out beside them and from small wells built for one type each. "Known answer" compares the page with the list of discrepancies planted in the made-up set. "Independent calculation" answers come from sverka_ref.py: separate code in Python that finds the discrepancies on its own and works out TVD by numerical integration, sharing no code with this page.
