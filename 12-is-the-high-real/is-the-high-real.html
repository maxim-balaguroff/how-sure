Is the high real?

A dolerite sill lies above a high on the depth map. Seismic waves cross the sill faster than the rock around it, so everything below arrives early and looks higher than it is. The page tells you how much of the high the sill alone can make, how that changes with the host rock, and how sure the sill velocity has to be.

Why it matters in petroleum work: a high under a sill can be a trap, or it can be the sill seen through a velocity model that does not know it is there. Drilling a high that is half velocity costs a well. Sills in trap provinces are common and can be hundreds of metres thick.

2. How sure is the sill velocity?

Sill velocitym/sThickness, mPull-up at the target, mShare of the high

Seismic measures the sill in time, top to base. The thickness is that time × velocity ÷ 2, so a velocity 15 % too high makes the sill 15 % too thick. In one published case the thickness of a sill came out between about 117 and 187 m for velocities of 4.5 to 6.0 km/s (Magee et al., 2019); the page does not repeat their full conversion, it shows the same effect with one rule.

How it works

  • Rays go straight down. Across the sill the wave spends 2t / Vs instead of 2t / Vh, so everything below arrives earlier by 2t (1/Vh − 1/Vs): the pull-up in two-way time.
  • A depth map made without the sill converts that early time with the host velocity, so the target comes out higher by the pull-up × Vh / 2 = t (1 − Vh / Vs). The overburden above the sill cancels: it is the same with and without the sill.
  • Seismic gives the sill in time, τ = 2t / Vs. Read with another velocity V, its thickness becomes τ V / 2 and the estimated pull-up (τ / 2)(V − Vh).
  • With the sill in the velocity model at the wrong velocity, the target moves by (τ / 2) × the velocity error: a well with a sonic log narrows that error.

Rules it uses

  • Share of the high: the pull-up at the target ÷ the height of the high.
  • Verdict: at least 50 % of the high could be the sill: red; 20 to 50 %: amber; under 20 %: green. A sill slower than its host pushes the horizon below it down and makes no pull-up: green. The flanks beside it can then look relatively high; the page does not judge them.
  • Host presets: clastic 3,000 m/s, the strata above one logged sill (Magee et al., 2019); rock salt 4,750 m/s, the middle of 4.5 to 5.0 km/s; dolomite 5,500 m/s, an estimate chosen close to the sill velocity, since dolerite and dolostone look alike on seismic (Svensen et al., 2018), inside 2.5 to 6.5 km/s (UBC EOSC 350 table).
  • The 50 % and 20 % thresholds are my conventions.

Limits

  • Straight vertical rays and flat layers. Under a sill narrower than the seismic Fresnel zone, the pull-up is smeared and smaller; at the sill edges, ray bending distorts the image. The page gives the full pull-up under the middle of a wide sill; a narrower sill and the smoothing of seismic imaging give less.
  • A sill of even thickness under the whole map lifts everything below it by the same amount and makes no high. The pull-up becomes relief only where the sill lies under the crest and is thinner, slower or absent under the flanks; the page takes the case of no sill under the flanks.
  • Velocity models from stacking velocities or tomography often hold part of the sill, smeared over a thicker interval. The page assumes none of it is in the model, so for the velocity you enter its figure is the largest.
  • One sill, one host velocity. Stacked sills add up; a host that changes velocity with depth needs its own interval velocities.
  • The drawing in the lower panel puts the high under the sill on purpose: that is the case the page tests. A high offset from the sill is not made by it.
  • The page checks one cause of a false high. Other causes (salt, shallow channels, statics) need their own checks.

Each check compares the page with an answer worked out another way: by hand from the relations above, or by an independent calculation that adds up two-way time cell by cell through a velocity log with a slow overburden and converts time back to depth by bisection, instead of the page's closed forms (physics_ref.py in the repository).
